## Method and evidence classes

Each work was opened on a primary page (arXiv abstract page with full version history, PMLR or USENIX or Crossref record, or the paper itself). For 18 works I read the paper text: the PDF was fetched with the fetch tool, which keeps the binary, and I extracted the text locally with `pdftotext` and searched it for the specific claim. This is better than the summaries the earlier agents used, because I could grep for the exact passage.

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| FULL | the text relevant to our claim was read and checked |
| ABSTRACT | the abstract or listing page was read; the body was not checked against any specific claim |
| META | bibliographic metadata only (Crossref, a publisher or proceedings page, a search-result page) |
| NOT FOUND | no primary page found |

Verdicts: **correct** (the claim matches the source), **misattributed** (the claim is right but credited to the wrong work, or the wrong authors, title or venue), **overstated** (the source says less than our note), **not found** (no primary source for the claim or venue).

Limits of what I did:
- The page-fetch tool returns a summary from a small model, so quotes were not available from web pages; for papers I used the extracted text instead. Pages that I could only read through the summariser (arXiv abstract pages, release pages, raw engine files) are marked as such in the evidence column.
- Engine source facts (SGLang v0.5.22, vLLM v0.32.0) were read a second time through the summariser (no verbatim code). They agree with the earlier agent's reading, so the two reads are independent but both summariser-mediated.
- The "year" of the GitHub release dates was not visible on the release pages; only day and month (19 Oct for SGLang v0.5.22, 18 Oct for vLLM v0.32.0), both marked "Latest".
- Where I give a number from a paper I read it in the extracted text, except where noted.
