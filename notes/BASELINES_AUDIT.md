**Numbers and minimal repairs.** The directly exposed claims are `notes/LOG.md:80,95,105` (P2: trapped 5/5, recovered 0/3; PEEK congested throughput 1.01 times all-miss capacity) and `notes/OUTLINE_prefix_meta.md:9,31-33,54,59` (PEEK assigned the FCFS asymptote and presented as a deployed-mechanism comparison). Keep those historical outcomes, but label them **FCFS queued-session protection ablations**. An optional warm-FIFO/cold-FIFO policy is the smallest correction to the omitted scheduler under the current workload abstraction. A separate optional token-length score isolates the eviction-score difference. These can weaken the claimed advantage of caps over PEEK, but do not change static-cap cells, the identity lambda < mu(N), or the direct evidence that caps restore this simulator's good state. The listed directions are mechanisms/inferences, not measured audit rerun effects.

**Opt-in implementation.** `kind: peek_singleton` adds the scheduler's singleton projection, including equal-arrival lane ties and the source EMA. `protect_queued: rank_length` adds the plain demand-times-depth eviction projection with LRU ties and rejects non-LRU compositions. The latter score uses the cached footprint, not the next request's larger prompt. Both switches are independent; the combined cell is **singleton scheduling plus plain-score eviction**, not the primary release's cluster-score stack. `tests/test_peek_audit.py` adds nine focused cases, including source-derived tied-arrival stride outcomes and a short invariant-checked integration run. Execution results are in the Measured results section.

**Verdict.** The comparison does not stand as a released-PEEK comparison. It remains a valid result about FCFS with queued-session soft preference, plus a hard-pin counterfactual. In particular, the mathematical FCFS conclusion survives but cannot be transferred to a scheduler that explicitly prioritizes warm requests and clusters. The opt-in singleton projection can quantify the missing warm-first effect without changing the simulator's default path; full PEEK evaluation still requires shared-prefix topology and its host engine's allocation semantics.

### Material differences, affected claims, and minimal changes

1. **Waiting-pressure trigger and victim key.** The round-3 P3 conclusion that eager release fails (`notes/LOG.md:81–83`) cannot be presented as a failure of the released preview: it tests a different pressure rule. The blocking values `ttl20=1.65`, `ttl5=2.58`, `ttl10=3.32` times lambda_miss (`notes/LOG.md:97–102`) and cold/burst recovery fractions can move if pins are broken at otherwise-idle failures. Relative to eager, preserving pins while work runs should retain more reusable KV and may raise congested throughput; relative to old idle, eliminating idle waiting adds immediate service but loses future hits, so the net direction is not determined without paired reruns. Latest-expiry versus latest-program victims has no universal direction. Minimal change: add a new mode reproducing only the released waiting-allocation branch: release one latest-expiry pin per failed idle pass, including own-program pins; retry in the simulator without charging nonexistent CPU scheduling time; retain all current defaults. Do not label this a full preview/paper implementation.
2. **TTL/eligibility and missing adaptive paper estimator.** All three blocking throughput ratios and the outline's tuned-TTL portion of the 2.2–3.3x statement (`notes/OUTLINE_prefix_meta.md:11`) are properties of configured synthetic pins, not the authors' 2-second conditional preview or learned paper TTL. With the synthetic mean tool gap of 5 seconds, a one-class preview mapping would eventually stop most pinning; losing the implicit admission throttle likely lowers the tuned 5/10-second throughput gains toward program-FCFS, but workload classes and cost-model effects preclude a guaranteed direction. Minimum scientifically valid change is explicit labels plus a separate tool-identity/observed-gap interface before implementing estimator switches. A global-mean switch would silently invent one tool class and is not an unambiguous fidelity fix.
3. **Final-turn cache reclamation.** This shared simplification can affect live-hit rates, `mu_infinity`, and the numeric best-cap region; keeping dead KV generally increases eviction pressure, so the default can be optimistic, though no strict sign follows for every throughput reading. Use the existing `engine.free_kv_on_session_end: false` as a separately labeled sensitivity. No new code is necessary; changing baseline defaults would break the requested historical identity.
4. **Engine integration/running preemption.** These can move all Continuum comparisons and the best cap; full reservation may exaggerate admission crowd-out, while recomputation on preemption can reduce throughput. The net difference is unidentifiable from source inspection. Round 5's low preemption rate and stable static-cap outcome (`notes/LOG.md:173–194`) concern FCFS/LPM engines, not a tested TTL composition: dynamic modes currently reject protection. The smallest honest action is retain that rejection and state the scope. A correct implementation needs program-aware preemption, shared references, dynamic pin capacity accounting, and parity tests; it is not a small fix.
5. **Eviction recency at pin release.** The same TTL throughput ratios and recovery counts can move because newly released KV is younger in the upstream free queue than in our idle LRU. The source policy can preserve the released cache for another turn at the expense of other idle caches; the net throughput sign is not guaranteed. Implemented a separate opt-in `ttl_refresh_lru_on_unpin: true` flag moving TTL-released idle KV to the newest eviction position on expiry, eager/eager_fit release, deadlock release, and the new waiting-pressure release. The default is false; true requires a TTL, and non-boolean values are rejected. The idle breaker changes recency only when it actually removes a TTL pin, so queued-only protection is unaffected. It does not change admission/eviction-time pin removal because that KV leaves idle altogether. The 24 focused cases in `tests/test_ttl_recency_audit.py` cover validation, expiry's changed eviction victim, each pressure mode, continued waiting protection, queued-only releases, and admission without a recency operation. All 24 pass (see the Execution record); the measured effect of the switch is none (Measured results). Keep page-tail ordering an explicit abstraction.

**Verdict.** The simulator evidence for a tuned synthetic blocking-pin mechanism and the independent static-session-cap result remains evidence about those modeled policies. The named comparison does not yet establish that Continuum's paper mechanism fails or achieves the quoted 1.65/2.58/3.32 recovery-throughput ratios. The released code has both a materially different pressure policy and a conditional 2-second estimator, and it openly omits the paper estimator. A paired rerun of the small pressure correction can determine whether that particular discrepancy changes the reported conclusion; it cannot validate the complete paper policy. The public preview and v7 prose should be reported as separate sources rather than choosing whichever pressure behavior matches the old result.

Open questions for authors/artifact follow-up: Is a full v7 implementation available separately? Is the latest-expiry, idle-only preview release intentional or superseded by section 5.2? What exact queue-delay window and memoryfulness estimator are used in the full release? Do parsed final-step flags always match program termination? No external messages were sent.

The short phrase establishing the missing primitive is that pause is "applied only at well-defined boundaries between generation steps and tool execution" (Section 4.3). Its victim selection and resume policy are not specified. Ye's later [technical article](https://yezhisheng.me/zh/post/concur/), paragraphs under agent-level admission, also emphasizes preserving existing agents by stopping new admissions. That supports the gate interpretation for admission continuity but does not resolve how the paper's separate pause/resume primitive changes an already-admitted cohort after a window cut. It would be unjustified to invent those details and call the result author-faithful.

**Numbers at risk and minimal corrective work (all directions below are inferences, not rerun measurements).**

1. Boundary pause/resume can change the `CONCUR = 3.03 lambda_miss` closed-N=300 measurement (`notes/LOG.md:90-102`), cold recovery and burst outcomes summarized as 3/3 and 5/5 (`85`), and CONCUR's contribution to the `2.2-3.3x` claim (`notes/OUTLINE_prefix_meta.md:11`). Promptly reducing an overlarge active set should reduce transient contention and can improve recovery time/hit rate; pausing too far below the throughput peak or resuming cold sessions can instead reduce throughput. The sign of the long-run 3.03 change is not identifiable without the missing policy. Minimal implementation: an opt-in boundary-pausing controller with distinct pending-new/paused-existing sets, a defined active-set count, victim/resume order, and unchanged in-flight requests. This is not a safe small fix: lifecycle bookkeeping and the unspecified choices materially determine the answer.
2. Signal definitions can move the same 3.03 and recovery figures by changing cap trajectories. Materialized-token usage is no larger than full reservation in the current model, so it can delay cuts or allow increases; near a thrashing cliff, that can lower recovery throughput even while raising instantaneous occupancy. A token-weighted hit signal may exceed a request-hit fraction when long reused prefixes dominate, delaying decreases; workload changes can reverse that comparison. Minimal implementation: opt-in usage/hit collectors with explicit denominators and smoothing. Author confirmation or released code is needed before identifying either as a fidelity fix.
3. The 10 s interval, initial 28, minimum 4 and rounding alter which side of the measured cap cliff the controller visits. Faster/more aggressive cuts can improve escape from congestion but can also leave an underfilled engine; starting lower can prevent early oversubscription but prolong gate delay. There is no defensible unconditional sign. They can move 3.03, latency/recovery counts, and the inference that the adaptive cap is robust. Minimal work is a sensitivity config over the existing parameters; it would measure assumptions rather than repair a known author mismatch. Do not replace them with guessed constants.
4. FIFO/FCFS composition can move the same CONCUR quantities if the author's backend prioritizes resident prefixes or if resume priority favors existing agents. Resident-first order would be expected to improve useful reuse in an otherwise matched state, whereas fairness or paused-agent switching can cost it; dynamic throughput has no monotonic guarantee. Minimal work is a separately labeled order-composition sensitivity (`kind: lpm` is already supported), followed by a released-scheduler check. It is not evidence that the authors used LPM.

These issues do **not** alter the static-cap identity or existing static-cap measurements. The round-4 3/3 recovery at caps 24/28 and 0/3 at 32/36/40 (`notes/LOG.md:131`), round-5 cap-20 recovery 3/3 (`189`), and the static identity in `notes/OUTLINE_prefix_meta.md:12` are separate from CONCUR's unspecified adaptive controller. Also, the preregistration explicitly evaluates P5 on the fallback `aimd_cap` only, with `concur_cap` an extra row (`configs/prefix_meta_mech_prereg.yaml:78-81,162-164`); the LOG's combined AIMD/CONCUR wording should not turn that into a registered CONCUR validation.

**Changes/reruns.** None for CONCUR: the configured Eq. 1 law and constants need no correction; the material differences require unspecified lifecycle/signal/scheduler details. The historical numbers above were re-measured unchanged, together with four sensitivity rows, in the Measured results section. Keep this row labeled a CONCUR-equation session-gate surrogate.

**Verdict.** The evidence that a well-chosen session cap stabilizes this simulator stands independently. The stronger assertion that the CONCUR implementation has been reproduced, or that 3.03 is the deployed mechanism's recovery threshold, does not stand: only its published control equation and numerical thresholds are matched, while lifecycle contraction and metric semantics remain unresolved. Retain the qualitative cap result and narrow the mechanism comparison to the tested surrogate until author code or sufficient operational details become available.

### Verdicts

- **PEEK: the comparison needs narrowing, and its number changes.** What stands: queued-session protection, with or without the released scheduler's warm-first ordering, does not restore stability in this private-prefix model (cold start 0/3 at every multiple from 1.1x up at the 30000 s horizon, burst recovered 0/5 with 3 to 5 of 5 trapped). What does not stand as stated: "PEEK-like protection is indistinguishable from FCFS at 1.01x". The released ordering, projected onto singleton groups, lifts mu(300) by 15% (+0.152 +- 0.015, 10 of 10 seeds) to 1.176, equal to plain LPM (difference +0.001 +- 0.014), and moves the cold-start threshold from between 0.9x and 1.0x to between 1.0x and 1.25x (2/3 at 1.1x). The eviction-score difference has no effect here. So PEEK belongs with the LPM-class orderings (about 1.17x), far below static and adaptive caps (about 3.0x) and tuned pins (2.6x to 4.4x). Label the old rows "FCFS queued-session protection ablation" and the audited row an ordering-and-score projection that says nothing about shared-prefix traffic.
- **Continuum: the comparison changes for tau = 20 s and for the statement that eager release fails; the pin result is otherwise strengthened.** Under the released preview's waiting-allocation rule the three pins give 2.58 / 3.51 / 4.38 x lambda_miss (seeds 0-9), monotone in tau, instead of 2.59 / 3.32 / 1.72. The tau = 20 s failure (1.65x with 3 seeds, cold recovery only to 1.25x, burst recovered 0/5) came from the old rule waiting for pin expiry with an idle GPU; with the preview's rule that row is the best (4.38x, cold recovery to 4.0x, burst 5/5). tau = 5 s is unchanged and tau = 10 s gains 5.7% (10 of 10 seeds). The eager rows (about 1.16x) test a rule the released preview does not contain, so the P3 sentence "eager unpin fails" cannot be read as a result about the released system. The release-time recency switch is immaterial (all differences within 0.1% of the value). The 2.2x-3.3x range quoted for tuned TTLs becomes 2.6x-4.4x. This remains the configured synthetic pin with the preview's pressure rule, not the preview itself (no 2-second tool-conditional eligibility, no estimator, no running-request preemption), and the dead-KV engine option does not change it.
- **CONCUR: the comparison stands only as a "CONCUR-equation session-gate surrogate", with one signal caveat.** The 3.03x is reproduced (3.009 +- 0.081 over 10 seeds) and is insensitive to backend order, control interval (5 s, 20 s) and initial window (8, 56): every paired-difference interval contains zero. Cold-start recovery reaches 2.0x-2.5x and bursts recover 5/5 in every variant. It is not robust to the usage definition: with finished sessions' KV left resident, 12 of 18 cells stall at cap 4 and about 0.93x. Until the authors' usage metric is known, report 3.0x as the surrogate's throughput under a usage that counts live sessions' KV only, not as the deployed mechanism's threshold.

Not measured: the shared-prefix behaviour of PEEK, Continuum's tool-conditional 2-second eligibility and estimator, running-request preemption, CONCUR's boundary pause/resume. The recovery counts rest on 3 cold-start seeds and 5 burst seeds, so the 10-seed curve is the better-powered evidence. All results are for the private-prefix workload of `configs/prefix_meta.yaml`, with `mu(300)` standing in for `mu_infinity`.

## Overall verdict and unresolved questions

The static-cap evidence is untouched by the audit: the cap identity and every static-cap cell use no code path that changed. The named-system comparison as measured now: PEEK-like about 1.0x-1.2x (it was 1.01x, and it is an LPM-class ordering once the released scheduler is projected), Continuum pins 2.6x-4.4x (tau 20 s no longer fails), CONCUR-equation caps about 3.0x (robust to the unspecified constants, not to the usage definition). The ranking "PEEK-like well below pins and caps" survives. The sub-claims that PEEK equals FCFS, that a 20 s pin fails and that eager release fails do not survive as statements about the named systems.

Remaining questions, unchanged by the runs: PEEK's unused no-sharing guard and shared-prefix cluster scoring; Continuum's full-v7 release, whether the preview's idle-only pressure release is intended, and its estimator; whether CONCUR has a later artifact specifying contraction, telemetry, interval, initial window and resume order (the ICML and OpenReview pages could not be fetched). No author was contacted.
