## Title

Thrashing Returns: Metastable Failures in Prefix-Cached Agent Serving

## Authors

Akshat Valse, Ali Jannesari

(Department of Computer Science, Iowa State University, Ames, IA, USA. Corresponding author: avalse@iastate.edu. Ali Jannesari: jannesar@iastate.edu. The authors should confirm both addresses.)

## Abstract (form)

```text
Prefix caching keeps an LLM agent session's KV across tool calls, so a hit prefills only new tokens. This can make the high-hit state metastable, in the physics sense. In a pre-registered paired test on SGLang 0.5.22 (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, one A100, 300k-token pool) at 1.35 and 1.65 times the measured all-miss capacity, a burst of 200 sessions left 6 of 6 runs at zero hits to the end, 7500 s later (5 trapped by the registered rule); without it 6 of 6 stayed good, and a client-side cap of 20 drained it in 6 of 6. The test also passed with SGLang's own 955,757-token pool and, registered, on vLLM 0.32.0 (a frozen mechanism check failed there); on an H200 the all-miss capacity rose 2.7-fold, the peak 1.6-fold. In registered rescue tests that cap (64 with SGLang's pool), engaged in an established trap, rescued 9 of 9 runs with no session shed; released after the drain, every run stayed good at the same load, uncapped controls at zero hits. Registered tests at 1.65 times put the basin boundary between bursts of 40 and 60. We derive closed-population throughput $\mu(n)$ from first principles, with misses swelling running requests' KV and nothing fitted to any $\mu(n)$, hit or trap data (another GPU or engine takes one speed factor): its all-miss onset is the sessions in tool calls plus one pool of running sessions, a uniformly faster GPU lowers its peak ratio, and exogenous-gap models without running KV cannot reach zero hits. In sample it matches SGLang's peaks within 10% but misses vLLM's earlier cliff; its frozen predictions held for the rescue, both basin rules (the first on runs begun before freezing) and a doubled A100 pool, not for a halved pool's peak and edge, the H200 curves' tails, 4 of 9 release times or one seed's basin probabilities; four tests are pending. Rates are nominal; registered results use one model, one GPU and synthetic sessions (exponential tools, no shared prefixes).
```

## Categories

- **Primary: cs.DC** (Distributed, Parallel, and Cluster Computing). The paper is about LLM serving engines (SGLang, vLLM) and their admission and caching behaviour under load. The closest serving work it compares with (PEEK, CONCUR, MISA-T, VAMP, XPerf) is listed there.
- **Cross-list: cs.PF** (Performance). The core of the paper is performance modelling and measurement: closed-population throughput curves, a first-principles model of the thrashing curve (Sec. 6), a fluid model of the open system (Sec. 3), metastability and recovery thresholds. The queueing and metastability work it builds on (Alvaro et al.; Yao et al.) is in cs.PF.
- **Cross-list: cs.OS** (Operating Systems). The mechanism is classical OS thrashing: working sets, KV eviction, multiprogramming-level load control. ThunderAgent and Continuum, which treat agent KV as an OS memory problem, are in cs.OS.
- Not cs.LG or cs.AI: the paper has no learning method, and moderators may remove such a cross-list.
