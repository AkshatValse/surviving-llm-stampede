The theory of \S\ref{sec:trap} explains and quantifies a phenomenon that we tested, independently of Lu et al., under
registered rules (Table~\ref{tab:evidence}). Each registered test fixed its rates, cap and decision rule by formula from earlier
measurements of the same cell (the closed curve $\mu(n)$, the all-miss rate with caching disabled and the from-empty edge), and
each arm ran in its own job on a fresh server, with the arms of a seed sharing arrival times, turn counts and tool times. Over the
five paired tests the burst left the engine at zero hits to the end of every one of its 30 BURST runs, 7500\,s after the burst, at
rates that the engine carried in the good state for the full 8700\,s of every NOBURST run; the registered slope rule called 23 of
them trapped. Post hoc, the plug-in of \S\ref{sec:validation} puts four of the other seven below the rule's threshold and two
within $10^{-4}$\,/s of it. On an H200 at the 300k pool, the all-miss capacity rose 2.71-fold and
the peak of $\mu(n)$ only 1.63-fold, so the window narrowed to $(1,1.3)\,\lref$, too narrow for the registration rule to place a
test; doubling the pool reopened it to $(1,2.5)\,\lref$ (\S\ref{sec:h200}).

\paragraph{What the record does not show.} The registered tests use synthetic sessions (random tokens, exponential 5\,s tool
times, geometric turn counts, no shared prefixes) and dense 7--8B models on one GPU per engine; all but two use Qwen2.5-7B on the
A100. The vLLM registration reached GitHub only after two of its jobs had started, so we call that test registered, not
pre-registered. The 300k H200 cell is a measurement, not a registered test, and its closed-curve values for $n\le32$ were seen by
the first author before the model's freeze. Some predictions are blind by access only, and the timeline of every registration and
prediction, with its slips, is in Appendix~\ref{app:timeline}. No registration is in a third-party registry.
