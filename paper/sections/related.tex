\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Thrashing, caching and metastability.} The thrashing collapse and the working-set remedy are Denning's~\citep{denning1968thrashing,denning1968working}, and the decomposition analysis of thrashing instability and the choice of a multiprogramming level are classical (\S\ref{sec:background}); LRU's failure on loops is classical~\citep{chou1985evaluation,smaragdakis1999eelru}, as is its weakness on weak-locality patterns~\citep{jiang2002lirs}. Metastable failures were named by Bronson et al.~\citep{bronson2021metastable}, surveyed by Huang et al.~\citep{huang2022metastable} and modelled as Markov chains by Alvaro et al.~\citep{alvaro2025formal}; Gibbens, Hunt and Kelly's networks with random alternative routing, stabilized by trunk reservation, are the closest classical analogue of a cap removing a congested regime~\citep{gibbens1990bistability}.

\paragraph{Queueing theory of LLM serving.} Ao et al.\ give fluid-guided scheduling with threshold admission~\citep{ao2025wait} and show that in a decode-only model, which abstracts away prefix reuse, the eviction-free fixed point can be unstable, with rate-limited admission and length mixing as remedies; its benchmark GPU experiments disable prefix caching~\citep{ao2026service}. Dai et al.\ prove throughput optimality of work-conserving policies without cache state, and give a cyclic-routing example where work-conserving scheduling fails~\citep{dai2025throughput}; Nie et al.\ give stability conditions under KV memory constraints for single requests~\citep{nie2026queueing}; Dong and Cao study flow-controlled admission~\citep{dong2026flow}. Yao et al.\ derive a mean-field LRU hit ratio for multi-turn conversations with exogenous gaps between turns~\citep{yao2026multiturn}; the feedback from load to gap length to hit rate is the loop studied here.

\paragraph{Prefix and KV mechanisms for agents.} PEEK protects prefixes needed by queued requests and adds a cache-aware scheduler~\citep{xie2026peek}; its ``scheduling-induced thrashing'' is LPM breaking ties by arrival order, not the 128-request fallback. Continuum pins KV across tool calls with a TTL and reports mean tool times of 925\,ms on SWE-Bench (standard deviation 3550\,ms) and 1923\,ms on BFCL~\citep{li2025continuum,patil2025bfcl}. CONCUR controls the number of active agents with AIMD and reports SGLang's hit rate falling from 77.7\% at batch 32 to 35.4\% at 40~\citep{chen2026concur}. ThunderAgent pauses programs whose memory demand exceeds the KV capacity and reports collapse without it under fixed concurrency~\citep{kang2026thunderagent}. CacheWise evaluates coding agents with sessions started together~\citep{tiwari2026cachewise}; XPerf, a benchmark, sees hit rates fall toward saturation on rate up-sweeps~\citep{wang2026xperf}. Other systems manage idle KV across tool calls or workflow steps by offloading, prefetching or retention~\citep{abhyankar2024infercept,pan2025kvflow,xia2026mori,bian2025tokencake,zhang2026cachescout,liu2026askthetool}, schedule agent programs~\citep{luo2026agentix,lin2024parrot}, cache conversation state hierarchically~\citep{gao2024cachedattention,yu2025pensieve,qin2025mooncake} (Mooncake's scheduler also rejects requests early under overload), or make locality-aware scheduling fair~\citep{cao2025dlpm}. Eviction-order work~\citep{pan2026rrevict,wu2026randomization,liu2026fancy} changes which idle prefix is evicted; RR-Evict assumes that the active trajectories fit in the cache, and Liu et al.\ replay traces without queueing feedback. Close neighbours we know only from their abstracts: Metronome calls a KV-exhaustion collapse in speech serving metastable, a different mechanism without prefix reuse \todo{Metronome, arXiv:2607.02640}; MISA-T admits sessions adaptively by KV usage \todo{MISA-T, arXiv:2608.11152}; and AgentServeSim and ServeTwin simulate agent serving with KV retained across tool gaps \todo{arXiv:2606.09613, 2610.02732; verify and add to refs.bib}.

\paragraph{Position.} Hit-rate collapse with too many active agents is published~\citep{chen2026concur,kang2026thunderagent,wang2026xperf,tiwari2026cachewise}, as is thrashing caused by scheduling order~\citep{xie2026peek}, and session-aware admission is deployed~\citep{kang2026thunderagent} \todo{NVIDIA Dynamo post}; we claim neither. To our knowledge no prior work on LLM serving shows the open-system, history-dependent version (a good state that persists from empty, a burst that leaves the system congested, and a cap that prevents it) against a no-burst control with a pre-registered rule on a real engine; in general distributed systems, the cache-loss version is Bronson et al.'s look-aside-cache case~\citep{bronson2021metastable}. Extending the models of Ao et al.~\citep{ao2026service} or Yao et al.~\citep{yao2026multiturn} so that inter-turn gaps depend on load would cover our setting; neither does so.
