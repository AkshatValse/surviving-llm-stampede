\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Thrashing, caching and metastability.} The thrashing collapse and the working-set remedy are Denning's~\citep{denning1968thrashing,denning1968working}, and the decomposition analysis of thrashing instability and the choice of a multiprogramming level are classical (\S\ref{sec:background}); LRU's failure on loops is classical~\citep{chou1985evaluation,smaragdakis1999eelru}, as is its weakness on weak-locality patterns~\citep{jiang2002lirs}. Metastable failures were named by Bronson et al.~\citep{bronson2021metastable}, studied in production incidents and modelled by Huang et al.~\citep{huang2022metastable}, analysed with queueing models, simulation and emulation by Isaacs et al.~\citep{isaacs2025analyzing} and with continuous-time Markov chains by Alvaro et al.~\citep{alvaro2025formal}, predicted with a queueing model for replicated storage by Habibi et al.~\citep{habibi2024msf}, and attributed to destabilizing feedback cycles by Farahbakhsh et al.~\citep{farahbakhsh2025modeling,farahbakhsh2026characterizing}. Gibbens, Hunt and Kelly's networks with random alternative routing are a classical case in which a state-dependent priority rule, trunk reservation, removes a congested mode and its hysteresis~\citep{gibbens1990bistability}; their modes are both stable, whereas our congested regime is transient, and the closer analogue of our cap is multiprogramming-level load control (\S\ref{sec:background}).

\paragraph{Queueing theory of LLM serving.} Ao et al.\ give fluid-guided scheduling with threshold admission~\citep{ao2025wait} and show that in a decode-only model, which abstracts away prefix reuse, the eviction-free fixed point can be unstable, with rate-limited admission and length mixing as remedies; its benchmark GPU experiments disable prefix caching~\citep{ao2026service}. In its open-arrival simulations, every load tested between the worst-cycle throughput, the analogue of our $\lmiss$, and the eviction-free capacity accumulated backlog. On our engine, backlog accumulated from an empty start only above the from-empty edge; inside our window the good state persisted from empty for the length of each run, and a burst moved the system out of it. Dai et al.\ prove throughput optimality of work-conserving policies without cache state, and give a cyclic-routing example where work-conserving scheduling fails~\citep{dai2025throughput}; Nie et al.\ give stability conditions under KV memory constraints for independent one-shot requests whose KV cache is freed at completion~\citep{nie2026queueing}; Dong and Cao study flow-controlled admission~\citep{dong2026flow}. Yao et al.\ derive a mean-field LRU hit ratio for multi-turn conversations with exogenous gaps between turns~\citep{yao2026multiturn}; the feedback from load to gap length to hit rate is the loop studied here.

\paragraph{Prefix and KV mechanisms for agents.} PEEK protects prefixes needed by queued requests and adds a cache-aware scheduler~\citep{xie2026peek}; its ``scheduling-induced thrashing'' is LPM breaking ties by arrival order, not the 128-request fallback. Continuum keeps a program's KV across a tool call for a time-to-live chosen by a cost model, which may be zero, and reports mean tool times of 925\,ms on SWE-Bench (standard deviation 3550\,ms) and 1923\,ms on BFCL~\citep{li2025continuum,patil2025bfcl}. CONCUR controls the number of active agents with AIMD and reports SGLang's hit rate falling from 77.7\% at batch 32 to 35.4\% at 40~\citep{chen2026concur}; MARS sets an admission window by AIMD from GPU KV pressure and CPU tool pressure, and reports that under heavier load its baselines' SLO-aware goodput collapses to near zero while their token throughput stays high~\citep{wang2026mars}. On vLLM, Yuan et al.\ report the prefix-cache hit rate falling from 98.85\% to 16.78\% as the maximum number of concurrent Terminal-Bench agents goes from 15 to 20~\citep{yuan2026agentic}, and VAMP calls the sharp rise in time to first token once reusable prefixes outgrow the KV pool the prefix-cache cliff, and measures stock vLLM at a 0.19\% prefix hit rate on a recorded SWE-bench agent replay~\citep{kim2026vamp}. ThunderAgent pauses whole programs when their KV demand, with programs in tool calls counted at a weight that decays with tool time, exceeds the capacity, and reports that baseline systems collapse in throughput as the number of parallel workflows grows, a closed population~\citep{kang2026thunderagent}. CacheWise evaluates coding agents with sessions started together~\citep{tiwari2026cachewise}; XPerf, a benchmark, sees hit rates fall toward saturation in fixed-rate runs at increasing rates~\citep{wang2026xperf}. Other systems manage idle KV across tool calls or workflow steps by offloading, prefetching or retention~\citep{abhyankar2024infercept,pan2025kvflow,xia2026mori,bian2025tokencake,zhang2026cachescout,liu2026askthetool,biswas2026sutradhara}, schedule agent programs~\citep{luo2026agentix,lin2024parrot}, cache conversation state hierarchically~\citep{gao2024cachedattention,yu2025pensieve,qin2025mooncake} (Mooncake's scheduler also rejects requests early under overload), admit, place or reserve capacity for agent sessions by tool readiness, KV residency or likely returns~\citep{li2026tao,jiang2026coda,meng2026talaria}, or make locality-aware scheduling fair~\citep{cao2025dlpm}. Eviction-order work~\citep{pan2026rrevict,wu2026randomization,liu2026fancy,pan2026scalesim} changes which idle state is evicted; RR-Evict assumes that the active trajectories fit in the cache, ScaleSim prefetches and evicts each agent's state by the estimated order of the agents' next requests, and Liu et al.\ replay traces without feedback from queueing to arrivals, as do Arya et al.\ for production Microsoft M365 agent traces, where LRU with a pooled budget of a tenth of the busiest hour's no-eviction demand makes 63\% of Deep Research calls recompute~\citep{arya2026residency}. MISA-T, for RL rollouts under a concurrency ceiling, reports vLLM Router's prefix-cache hit rate falling from 92.4\% to 4.5\% at a high ceiling, describes the positive feedback behind it, notes that at prefill saturation the request rate is bounded by prefill throughput over the mean uncached tokens per request, and interrupts the loop with an adaptive admission cap for new sessions derived from observed KV demand and overload pressure~\citep{hong2026misat}; it has no open arrivals, bursts or recovery test. Other close neighbours: Metronome calls a collapse in real-time speech-model serving metastable, in the sense that identical runs collapse or survive near a threshold, but there each session's KV stays pinned for the whole conversation until the pinned KV exhausts the pool, a different mechanism from eviction and recomputation of reusable prefixes~\citep{meng2026metronome}; and, known to us only from their abstracts, AgentServeSim simulates agent serving with KV retained across tool gaps~\citep{rajib2026agentservesim} and ServeTwin simulates multi-turn agent workloads while tracking KV-cache evolution~\citep{park2026servetwin}.

\paragraph{Position.} Hit-rate collapse with too many active agents is published~\citep{chen2026concur,kang2026thunderagent,wang2026xperf,yuan2026agentic,hong2026misat}, as are thrashing from repeated eviction and recomputation under concurrent sessions~\citep{tiwari2026cachewise,biswas2026sutradhara}, thrashing caused by scheduling order~\citep{xie2026peek} and the feedback that sustains it, between eviction, cold prefill and queueing under a concurrency ceiling~\citep{hong2026misat} and as a self-reinforcing eviction--recompute cascade under memory pressure~\citep{kim2026vamp}; session-aware admission is implemented or proposed~\citep{kang2026thunderagent,dhanani2026dynamo,hong2026misat,wang2026mars,li2026tao,jiang2026coda}; we claim none of these. To our knowledge no prior work on LLM serving shows the open-system, history-dependent version (a good state that persists from empty, a burst that leaves the system congested, and a cap that prevents it) against a no-burst control with a pre-registered rule on a real engine; in general distributed systems, the cache-loss version is Bronson et al.'s look-aside-cache case~\citep{bronson2021metastable}, whose persistence Farahbakhsh et al.\ attribute partly to the database serving its queue in arrival order~\citep{farahbakhsh2026characterizing}. Covering our setting would take multi-turn sessions and prefix reuse in the model of Ao et al.~\citep{ao2026service}, and in that of Yao et al.~\citep{yao2026multiturn} a finite server whose recompute cost feeds back into the gaps between turns; neither paper has these.
