The theory of \S\ref{sec:trap} explains a phenomenon that we tested, independently of Lu et al., under registered rules
(Table~\ref{tab:stampede}). This section summarizes those tests; Appendix~\ref{app:stampede} reports them in full.

\paragraph{Workload and engines.} A session has $T=\min(\mathrm{Geometric}(1/15),60)$ turns ($E[T]=14.76$). Turn $k$ sends a
prompt of $2000+650(k-1)$ tokens, the previous prompt plus 100 decoded tokens and 550 tokens of tool output, as random token ids
(no shared prefixes), and between turns the session waits for an exponential tool time of mean 5\,s. A turn \emph{hits} if more
than half of its prompt is cached. An open-loop client sends Poisson session arrivals to SGLang 0.5.22 (request-level FCFS with its
waiting-prefix refresh, LRU eviction) or vLLM 0.32.0, serving Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on one A100 80GB PCIe or
one H200 NVL, with the KV pool capped at 300k tokens (150k or 600k in the pool sweep; 955,757 tokens, SGLang's own, in the native
cell). Before every run the cache is flushed and checked with a canary, and each arm of a test runs in its own job on a fresh
server.

\paragraph{Calibration and the registered rule.} Each cell was calibrated before its test: the closed-population throughput curve
$\mu(n)$ (Stage~C), the all-miss session rate $\lref$ with the radix cache disabled (E0a) and the from-empty edge $\ledge$, the largest
rate at which runs from an empty engine stayed stable for 3000\,s (E0b). In the quasi-static model $\dot n=\lambda-\mu(n)$ the good
state is then metastable in the window $(\lref,\min(\mu^*,\ledge))$ (Appendix~\ref{app:qs}), and a registration placed the test rates
one and two thirds of the way across it and the cap at the smallest $n$ with $\mu(n)\ge0.95\mu^*$, by formula. The paired test E2
runs three arms per seed for 8700\,s, judged over $[5700,8700)$\,s, with the arms of a seed sharing arrival times, turn counts and
tool times, fixed before any run by the client's per-seed plan (Table~\ref{tab:stampede}). Its rule calls a BURST run trapped by tokens, not sessions, because a trapped engine prefills
at the all-miss token rate while completing sessions faster than $\lref$ (\S\ref{sec:validation}). Pre-registered and registered are defined in
Table~\ref{tab:stampede}; neither label says whether a prediction was blind, and no registration is in a third-party registry.

\paragraph{What the tests show.} Over the five paired tests the burst left the engine at zero hits to the end of every one of its
30 BURST runs, 7500\,s after the burst, at rates that the engine carried in the good state for the full 8700\,s of every NOBURST
run; the registered slope rule called 23 of them trapped (post hoc, the plug-in of \S\ref{sec:validation} puts four of the other seven
below the rule's threshold and two within $10^{-4}$\,/s of it). A cap within 5\% of
the measured peak of $\mu(n)$ prevented the trap in all 30 CAPPED runs of the five paired tests, at the price of admission delay at
the client (405--471\,s on average in E2). The registered dose response places the basin boundary between
bursts of 40 and 60 sessions in every seed (50 trapped 1 of 3). A tool slowdown with no burst can also trap the engine: lengthening
every tool call that starts in $[1200,2100)$\,s by 30\,s trapped 1 of 3 runs, so that registered rule (traps in 2 of 3) was not met,
and \S\ref{sec:validation} reports a longer slowdown registered later. On an H200 at the 300k pool, the all-miss capacity rose
2.71-fold and the peak of $\mu(n)$ only 1.63-fold, so the window narrowed to $(1,1.3)\,\lref$, too narrow for the registration rule
to place a test; doubling the pool reopened it to $(1,2.5)\,\lref$ (Appendix~\ref{app:entry} explains the shift). Stopping
arrivals (SHED) and a cap engaged inside an established trap (R1, R4) are the two exits of \S\ref{sec:control}.

\paragraph{The trap persists and outlives its trigger.} Among the runs before the blind tests, no FCFS run trapped by a burst or by a
tool slowdown left the trap on its own. Over 50 such runs, about 100 trapped hours (99--103\,h, depending on where the trap is taken
to start), no 300\,s window after the trap formed reached a follow-up hit fraction of 0.5; the highest was 0.16. Treating the runs as
independent, which shared seeds weaken, a one-sided 95\% bound on a run's probability of escaping within its observed span (at least
6100\,s) is 5.8\%. Stock LPM (SGLang's longest-prefix-match ordering), which leaves request-level FCFS while at most 128 requests wait, escaped in 4 of its 9 trapped runs, and 1 of
the 16 from-empty collapses (150k pool, 1.30~$\lref$) recovered within its 3000\,s run. In 16 stuck runs every trigger session had
left the system 558--6246\,s before the end, and the 727--15{,}453 turns completed after that had no hit in 15 runs (4 hits among
5{,}937 turns in the trapped TOOLSLOW run): the trap outlives its trigger, as Lu et al.\ also observed on
hardware~\citep{lu2026cachetrap}. In the 24 BURST runs of the four A100 paired tests we cannot say so: 3--30 burst sessions were still
present at the end. These counts are post hoc, computed after Lu et al.'s paper appeared (Appendix~\ref{app:dataonly}).

\paragraph{What the record does not show.} The tests use synthetic sessions and dense 7--8B models on one GPU per engine; all but
two use Qwen2.5-7B on the A100. The vLLM registration reached GitHub only after two of its jobs had started, so we call that test
registered, not pre-registered. The 300k H200 cell is a measurement, not a registered test; its closed-curve values for $n\le32$ were
seen by the cell's operating agent and the first author before the model's freeze, and no blind simulator prediction was made for
it. A fresh-seed replication of the
from-empty edge at 300k did not meet its rule (2.5~$\lref$ was stable in 3 of 3 fresh seeds; in realized rates the edge lies at
2.665--2.726~$\lref$). Some predictions for these tests are blind by access only, and the timeline of every registration and
prediction, with its slips, is in Appendix~\ref{app:stampede}.
