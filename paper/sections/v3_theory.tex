\section{Model F: a renewal law for the trap}\label{sec:trap}

The quasi-static reading of \S\ref{sec:intro}, whose curve has as its low end Lu et al.'s low-reuse departure rate
$D_{\mathrm{low}}$~\citep{lu2026cachetrap} and, in our earlier model, the all-miss capacity (Appendix~\ref{app:qs}), fails on our
engines (\S\ref{sec:validation}). Here we characterize the trap exactly, within an explicit idealization of the trapped engine.

\paragraph{Model F.} Let a session have $T$ turns, $G_k=P(T\ge k)$ for $k=1,\dots,K$ with $G_K>0$, and let turn $k$ cost $c_k>0$
GPU seconds when it misses. The costs come from an iteration-cost model fitted only to server-log timings
(Appendix~\ref{app:entry}): the miss prefill of turn $k$ plus the all-miss per-turn decode share. Write
\[
\lmiss=\Big(\sum_kc_kG_k\Big)^{-1},\qquad \rho=\lambda/\lmiss ,
\]
the all-miss capacity at the stationary turn mix and the load. Rates in this paper are multiples of $\lref$, the all-miss
session rate measured with the radix cache disabled (E0a, 32 sessions; 0.0715\sps{} for SGLang on the A100); $\lmiss$ is model~F's
value from the cost model, and $\lmiss/\lref=0.96$--$1.035$ across our cells. Model~F is a fluid single-server queue in which
\begin{enumerate}[(F1),leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item every turn misses, so turn $k$ costs $c_k$;
\item the server works at unit rate while anything is pending;
\item requests complete in the order in which they joined (request-level FCFS);
\item a completed turn $k<T$ rejoins the tail of the queue at once as turn $k+1$;
\item new sessions join as turn~1 at rate $\lambda>0$;
\item the state at time 0 is any finite pending mass with positive work.
\end{enumerate}
(F3)--(F4) make model~F a round robin over sessions with visit-dependent quanta $c_k$: a single-station re-entrant
line~\citep{kumar1993reentrant} in overload. A request that joins at time $u$ completes at $f(u)=u+V(u)$, where $V(u)$ is the
pending work; so $V$ is also the gap between two consecutive turns of a session. Call $u_0,\ u_{n+1}=f(u_n)$ a \emph{FIFO orbit}
and $\Delta_n=u_{n+1}-u_n$ its cycles. Let $N(t)$ count the sessions present and $V_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)$ be their total remaining
all-miss work, future turns included, and let $\bar k_c=\sum_kkc_kG_k/\sum_kc_kG_k$ be the cost-weighted mean turn index.

\begin{lemma}[the orbit recursion]\label{lem:recursion}
In model~F, while the queue is busy the remaining work changes at rate $\rho-1$, and along every FIFO orbit the cycles solve the
linear renewal recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal}
\Delta_n=\sum_{k=1}^{\min(n,K)}\lambda c_kG_k\,\Delta_{n-k}+h_n ,
\end{equation}
where $h_n=\sum_{k=n+1}^{K}c_kG_k\pi_{k-n}(u_0)/G_{k-n}$, with $\pi_j(u_0)$ the turn-$j$ mass pending at $u_0$, is the work
that the requests pending at $u_0$, and their later turns, bring in generation $n$; so $h_n\ge0$, $h_n=0$ for $n\ge K$ and
$\sum_nh_n=V_{\mathrm{tot}}(u_0)$. An orbit with $u_0<V(0)$ also satisfies, for $n\ge K-1$, the exact identity
$u_{n+1}=V_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)+\sum_k\lambda c_kG_ku_{n+1-k}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[every trapped state converges]\label{thm:trap}
In model~F let $\rho>1$, and let $z>1$ be the Perron root, $\lambda\sum_kc_kG_kz^{-k}=1$. From any initial state:
\begin{enumerate}[(a),leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item as $t\to\infty$ the gap grows linearly, $V(t)/t\to\gamma=z-1$; the backlog grows as $N(t)/t\to\alpha=\lambda(1-E[z^{-T}])$;
sessions complete at rate $D=\lambda E[z^{-T}]$; and the pending requests, and the requests served in each cycle, have the tilted
turn mix $\nu^z_k=G_kz^{-k}/\sum_jG_jz^{-j}$;
\item (affine law with a virtual origin) let $u^*=V_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)/(\rho-1)$ and let $r$ be the largest modulus of the other roots
of $x^K=\sum_k\lambda c_kG_kx^{K-k}$. Every FIFO orbit with $u_0<V(0)$ satisfies $u_n+u^*=Bz^n+O((r+\epsilon)^n)$ for every
$\epsilon>0$, with a constant $B$, so relative to $z^n$ the transient is $O(((r+\epsilon)/z)^n)$. Hence the gap is affine in time with
virtual origin $-u^*$, $V(u_n)=\gamma(u_n+u^*)+O((r+\epsilon)^n)$, and proportional to the remaining work,
$V(u_n)=\frac{\gamma}{\rho-1}V_{\mathrm{tot}}(u_n)+O((r+\epsilon)^n)$; when $r<1$ these errors vanish absolutely. A session that
arrives at $u$ with $T=m$ turns completes at $f^m(u)$, and $f^m(u)+u^*=z^m(u+u^*)(1+o(1))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[the classical parts]\label{prop:classic}
In model~F:
\begin{enumerate}[(a),leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item the queue empties if and only if $\rho<1$, at $V_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)/(1-\rho)$; with arrivals stopped ($\lambda=0$, outside (F5)) it
empties at $V_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)$;
\item if $\rho=1$, $V(t)\to V_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)/\bar k_c$ and $N(t)\to\lambda E[T]V_{\mathrm{tot}}(0)/\bar k_c$;
\item as $\rho\downarrow1$, $\gamma=(\rho-1)/\bar k_c+O((\rho-1)^2)$ and $\alpha/(\lambda-\lmiss)\to E[T]/\bar k_c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\noindent Part~(a) is classical in substance: at a single work-conserving station the remaining work obeys
$\dot V_{\mathrm{tot}}=\rho-1$ whatever the service order, which gives the fluid busy period, and its stochastic counterpart is
Kendall's branching description of the busy period~\citep{kendall1951some}. Part~(b) is the fluid counterpart of the state-space
collapse of a critically loaded multiclass FIFO feedback queue~\citep{reiman1988multiclass,dai1995multiclass}; we prove it from
Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion} with the discrete renewal theorem~\citep[Ch.~XIII]{feller1968introduction}. Part~(c) is an elementary expansion of
the Perron equation.

\begin{theorem}[youth premium]\label{thm:premium}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:trap}(a), $D/\lmiss=H(z)\,\Gamma(z)$, where
\[
H(z)=\frac{E[T]\,E[z^{-T}]}{\sum_kG_kz^{-k}},\qquad \Gamma(z)=\frac{E_\nu[c]}{E_{\nu^z}[c]},
\]
with $\nu_k\propto G_k$ the stationary and $\nu^z_k\propto G_kz^{-k}$ the trapped turn mix. $H$ depends only on the law of $T$:
$H=1$ for a geometric $T$ on $\{1,2,\dots\}$ ($K=\infty$; the formulas extend for $z>1$), and $H\in(1-q^K,1)$, non-increasing
in $z$, for $T=\min(\mathrm{Geometric}(1-q),K)$ with $K\ge2$. If $c$ is non-decreasing, $\Gamma\ge1$, with equality only for
constant $c$, and $\Gamma$ is non-decreasing in $z$. For that $T$ and a non-decreasing, non-constant $c$ with
$E_\nu[c]/c_1>1/(1-q^K)$, let $z_1$ solve $\Gamma(z_1)=1/(1-q^K)$; then $D>\lmiss$ for every $z\ge z_1$. Near the threshold,
$D/\lmiss-1=(\rho-1)(1-E[T]/\bar k_c)+O((\rho-1)^2)$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent In model~F the trapped server can thus complete sessions faster than its all-miss capacity while its backlog diverges, because
it serves a young backlog of short, cheap turns. The premium is not automatic: truncation lowers $H$, and with
$T=\min(\mathrm{Geometric}(1/15),60)$ and slowly growing costs $c_k=1+10^{-3}(k-1)$ it is negative at every load checked; for a
nearly deterministic $T$, $H$ can be well below 1 (\S\ref{sec:mirror}). For our workload ($c_{60}/c_1=17$--23), $E[T]/\bar k_c=0.60$--$0.62$
in all eight cells, and the premium is positive at every load we checked: proved by Theorem~\ref{thm:premium}'s monotonicity for
$\rho\ge1.04$, shown by a monotone bracket ($H$ falling, $\Gamma$ rising; evaluated in floating point, not interval arithmetic) on
$z\in[1+10^{-12},z_1]$, and only to first order below that (Appendix~\ref{app:trap}).

\begin{corollary}[the drain threshold is $\lmiss$, not the trapped throughput]\label{cor:trapthr}
In model~F at load $\lambda>\lmiss$, let the arrival rate change to $\lambda'$ at a time $t_0$ when the queue is busy. The
queue then empties if and only if $\lambda'<\lmiss$, after the busy period $V_{\mathrm{tot}}(t_0)/(1-\lambda'/\lmiss)$. If
$\lambda'>\lmiss$ the backlog grows as $\alpha(\lambda')t$; at $\lambda'=\lmiss$ the fluid backlog settles at a positive
level. In particular, whenever $D(\lambda)>\lmiss$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:premium}; for our workload at every load we checked), a
cut to any rate in $[\lmiss,D(\lambda))$, below the asymptotic trapped throughput, does not empty model~F's queue.
\end{corollary}

\begin{nremark}[invariance]\label{rem:trapinv}
$z$ solves $\rho\sum_k\omega_kz^{-k}=1$ with the cost-weighted turn mix $\omega_k=c_kG_k/\sum_jc_jG_j$. So $z$, $\gamma$, the
trapped composition, $\alpha/\lmiss$ and $D/\lmiss$ depend on a cell only through $\rho$, $\omega$ and the law of $T$; they are
unchanged by a uniform GPU speed-up at equal $\rho$. This needs proportional cost vectors; our H200 cell model is a uniform speed-up
of the A100's, and the remark is not a general hardware claim.
\end{nremark}

\paragraph{Proof idea.} FIFO and work conservation give $f(u)=V(0)+W(u)$, where $W(u)$ is the work that has joined since
time~0. FIFO maps the requests that join during one cycle onto the requests that complete during the next, so a session
whose first turn joined in $(u_{n-k},u_{n-k+1}]$ rejoins as turn $k$ in $(u_{n-1},u_n]$, and the turn-$k$ mass joining there
is $\lambda G_k\Delta_{n-k}$. Summing its work, $\Delta_n=W(u_n)-W(u_{n-1})$, turns the nonlinear fluid into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:renewal}, whose coefficients are nonnegative and sum to $\rho$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:recursion}). Tilting by $z^{-n}$ and a
finite-memory renewal lemma give Theorem~\ref{thm:trap}(a) and Proposition~\ref{prop:classic}(b); the exact identity gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:trap}(b); Proposition~\ref{prop:classic}(a) is a busy-period argument. Appendix~\ref{app:trap} gives complete
proofs, the stochastic counterpart and a sketch of the processor-sharing variant; \fpath{tests/test_theory_icml.py} checks most
statements numerically (Appendix~\ref{app:trap} lists which); independent AI-agent checks are recorded in \fpath{notes/THEORY_ICML_2026-11.md}, \S10.

\paragraph{What is new.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:renewal} is a discrete renewal equation~\citep{feller1968introduction} of Lotka--Leslie type:
the arrivals $\lambda\Delta_n$ play the role of births, $G_k$ of survival and $\lambda c_kG_k$ of net maternity, and the tilted mix
$G_kz^{-k}$ is Lotka's stable age distribution. Viewed as a steady solution (constant flows from the empty state), the self-similar
regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:trap} is the single-station case of the steady solutions of fluid FIFO re-entrant lines of Khanin et
al.~\citep{khanin2001steady}, which give the Perron equation, the gap slope $z-1$, the $z^{-k}$-tilted mix and the departure flow; they treat
deterministic stages, a closing remark extends their framework to a fixed fraction of the flow leaving at each node (our random
$T$), their normalization gives Remark~\ref{rem:trapinv}, and they leave open the uniqueness of non-steady trajectories. The processor-sharing
version of model~F has the Crump--Mode--Jagers structure of overloaded processor sharing, whose fluid shape converges to an invariant
one~\citep{grishechkin1992processor,jeanmarie1994transient,puha2006overloaded}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:premium} is the visit-indexed
analogue of its departure rate $\lambda E[e^{-\alpha_{\mathrm{PS}}S}]$ ($\alpha_{\mathrm{PS}}$ its growth exponent), which can exceed
$1/E[S]$~\citep{jeanmarie1994transient}. What we add, as far
as a targeted search found (\S\ref{sec:related}), is the derivation of the recursion along FIFO orbits, which carries every solution
from \emph{any} trapped state to the self-similar regime, geometrically in the generation index and with an exact virtual origin,
whereas a burst leaves the engine far from a steady solution; and the turn-count and cost-growth factors of the premium.
Corollary~\ref{cor:trapthr} is not new as mathematics: it is the operational reading of Theorem~\ref{thm:premium} with the classical
drain condition, its processor-sharing counterpart follows from~\citep{jeanmarie1994transient} in the same way, and Lu et al.\ state
that arrivals below their low-reuse departure rate drain the population~\citep{lu2026cachetrap}. What is new about it is its test on
the engine (\S\ref{sec:control}).

\paragraph{Model F is an idealization.} On the engine, (F1) is the measured zero hit fraction of the trap and (F2) its saturated
GPU. (F3) is request-level FCFS admission, up to the $M/E_S$ requests that run concurrently ($M$ the pool in tokens, $E_S$ the
service-weighted KV of a running turn; about 28 at 300k); Proposition~\ref{prop:A} (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) bounds the closed system at the stationary mix,
and the open trap exceeds $\lmiss$ only through its tilted mix. (F4) neglects the 5\,s tool time against cycles of 166--516\,s; adding
it changes the fluid's backlog slope, completion rate and mean served prompt over the window by less than 0.02\%. Model~F leaves out
concurrency, preemption (vLLM preempts), and the transitions into and out of the trap, where hits return. We do not prove that the
engine converges to model~F; its test is empirical (\S\ref{sec:validation}).

\paragraph{Why the trap does not heal.} Theorem~\ref{thm:trap}(a) makes the gap between a session's turns grow linearly, while the
trapped engine rewrites its pool at the all-miss token rate, every $R_M=M/\text{churn}\approx26$--78\,s in our cells (churn, the rate
at which new KV is written: uncached prompt plus completion tokens per second). Once the gap is several times $R_M$, as it is in
every BURST run of the first three registered tests (gap$/R_M=4.1$--9.7, Table~\ref{tab:gapR}), an idle session's KV is overwritten
long before it returns, so (F1) is self-consistent, and becomes more so as the trap ages. The pool need not be full: admitted
requests held 55--83\% of it on average in the trapped SGLang runs with 300k and 600k pools (the engine's gauge, which also counts
locked prefixes), and 85--94\% with the native pool and on
vLLM. This is a heuristic consistency argument, not part of the theorem: $R_M$ is a turnover scale, not a pathwise eviction bound. With
Poisson arrivals, Kendall's branching argument puts the probability that the all-miss queue ever empties at 0.35 for $\rho=1.01$ and
$4\times10^{-23}$ at 1.35, for a burst of 200 sessions into an empty engine and the remaining work 3000\,s later taken as realized
and equal to the fluid's (Appendix~\ref{app:trap}); these are not bounds, since hits return first.
