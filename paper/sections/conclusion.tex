\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Prefix caching turns multi-turn agent serving into a thrashing workload: the cache that makes follow-up turns cheap belongs to sessions idle in tool calls or waiting in the queue, and admission reclaims it. On SGLang with two pool sizes and on vLLM, at rates the engine carried in the good state for the full length of every no-burst run, a burst of 200 sessions moved the engine into an all-miss state that it did not leave within 7500\,s, while a client-side cap at the peak of $\mu(n)$ drained the same burst; two of these tests were pre-registered and the third registered, and all three pass by their registered rule, although on vLLM the frozen decision rule for the mechanism checks is not met. The same cap, engaged inside an established trap, returned the engine to the good state in 9 of 9 registered runs without shedding a session, and with the cap removed, once the client queues had stayed empty for 300\,s, the good state persisted at the same load while the uncapped controls stayed at zero hits: which state the engine is in depends on its history, not only on its load. Bursts of 25 and 40 sessions were absorbed and bursts of 60 and more trapped the engine in every seed, so the good state has a finite basin, whose boundary lies between 40 and 60 sessions at 1.65 $\lref$ and depends on the seed. In our first-principles model, misses swell running reservations and squeeze idle KV, and the queue sets the gap between a session's turns once every turn misses; no parameter is fitted to any $\mu(n)$, hit or trap measurement. It reproduces the SGLang curves (in sample) and the trapped states, and shows why exogenous-gap cache models without running KV cannot reach the measured zero hits. Before the results were read, it predicted the rescue, both basin tests' rules (BASIN-2's with probability 0.66), the curve with a doubled A100 pool and, in nominal multiples, the from-empty edge at that pool and on an H200 at two pools. It missed the H200's softer tail past the peak (at 600k also the 0.5-hit crossing, 4.4 sessions late), the peak height and edge with a halved A100 pool, 4 of the 9 rescue release times, one seed's basin probabilities and, on Llama-3.1-8B, the edge (a near miss, one grid step high) and a later-declared test of the tool-call term. On the H200 at 300k the peak ratio fell from 3.15 to 1.89, in the direction of the model's speed law, and the from-empty edge, which sets the window's upper end on both GPUs, fell to 1.3 $\lref$ (nominal). These results are for synthetic sessions with exponential 5\,s tool times and no shared prefixes; the paired tests of the 600k H200 and Llama-3.1-8B cells, SHED and TOOLSLOW are pending in this version. Operators should measure $\mu(n)$ for the workload they serve, cap admitted sessions at the smallest population within a few percent of its peak, accepting that a burst then waits at the client, and re-measure when the workload, pool, engine or GPU changes; a cap engaged after a collapse still restores the good state, but preventing the collapse costs no more client delay, and much less with the native pool.
