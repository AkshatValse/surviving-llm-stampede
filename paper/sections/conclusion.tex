\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Prefix caching turns multi-turn agent serving into a thrashing workload: the cache that makes follow-up turns cheap belongs to sessions idle in tool calls or waiting in the queue, and admission reclaims it. In our SGLang configuration the engine stayed in the good state from empty over 3000\,s at nominal rates up to twice the all-miss capacity (2.2 times in realized arrivals), and at nominal rates of 1.35 and 1.65 times that capacity the good state was metastable: in a pre-registered test a burst moved the engine into an all-miss state that it did not leave within 7500\,s, the no-burst arm stayed good and the capped arm recovered, and a simulator whose cost model was fitted only to server-log timing had predicted this outcome in predictions pushed to GitHub before the test was submitted. With the KV pool SGLang sizes itself, the cliff and the from-empty edge moved out, the edge to between nominal 5.5 and 6.5 times the all-miss capacity, and a second pre-registered test at 2.5 and 4.0 times passed at both rates with every run in its predicted state, as the simulator had predicted for somewhat higher rates. On vLLM 0.32.0 with the 300k-token pool, the engine's own closed curve and from-empty edge select the same window and cap as on SGLang; in exploratory runs without control arms it reached the same all-miss state after the same burst, and a registered paired test with all three arms was still running when this version was prepared (\S\ref{sec:vllm}). These results are for one model, one GPU type and synthetic sessions with exponential 5\,s tool times and no shared prefixes. Operators should measure the closed-population curve for the workload they serve, cap admitted sessions at the smallest population within a few percent of its peak, accepting that a burst then waits at the client, and re-measure when the workload changes.
