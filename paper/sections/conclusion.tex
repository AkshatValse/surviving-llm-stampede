\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Prefix caching turns multi-turn agent serving into a thrashing workload: the cache that makes follow-up turns cheap belongs to sessions idle in tool calls, and admission reclaims it first. In our SGLang configuration the engine stayed in the good state from empty up to twice the all-miss capacity over 3000\,s, and at 1.35 and 1.65 times that capacity the good state was metastable: in a pre-registered test a burst moved the engine into an all-miss state that it did not leave within 7500\,s, the no-burst and capped arms stayed good, and a timing-calibrated simulator had predicted this. With the KV pool SGLang sizes itself, the cliff and the from-empty edge moved out, the edge to between 5.5 and 6.5 times the all-miss capacity, and a second pre-registered test at 2.5 and 4.0 times passed in every run, as the simulator had predicted for somewhat higher rates. vLLM 0.32.0, tested without control arms, reached the same all-miss state after the same burst. These results are for one model, one GPU type and synthetic sessions. Operators should measure the closed-population curve for the workload they serve, cap admitted sessions near its peak, and re-measure when the workload changes.
