\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Prefix caching turns multi-turn agent serving into a thrashing workload: the cache that makes follow-up turns cheap belongs to sessions idle in tool calls or waiting in the queue, and admission reclaims it. On SGLang with two pool sizes and on vLLM, at rates the engine carried in the good state for the full length of every no-burst run, a burst of 200 sessions moved the engine into an all-miss state that it did not leave within 7500\,s, while a client-side cap at the peak of $\mu(n)$ drained the same burst; two of these tests were pre-registered and the third registered, and all three pass by their registered rule, although on vLLM the frozen decision rule for the mechanism checks is not met, because an online hit-rate detector also alarmed in two capped runs. In the trapped state the gap between a session's turns, mostly turn latency, is 4 to 10 times the time in which the pool is rewritten; in the good state it is 1 to 3\% of it. A first-principles model with no parameter fitted to any $\mu(n)$, hit or trap measurement (the H200 and vLLM cells take one speed factor fitted to their all-miss token rate) reproduces the SGLang curves and the trapped states and misses vLLM's earlier cliff; its predictions for a faster GPU, other pools, a second model, burst sizes and a cap rescue were frozen before their results were read by the agents that derived the model. On an H200 it predicted the 0.5-hit crossing (37.3 against about 38.9 sessions), the from-empty edge (1.39 times that GPU's all-miss capacity, inside the $[1.3,1.4)$ measured on the original runs) and the peak ratio (1.92 against 1.89, although the first author had seen the peak runs before the freeze), but not the soft tail past the peak, and the window there shrank to $(1,1.3)$ times that capacity, too narrow for a registered test; a registered burst-size test passed as predicted, with 25 sessions absorbed and 100 trapping the engine (the model's 100-session predictions are blind by access only); the other outcomes are pending in this version. These results are for synthetic sessions with exponential 5\,s tool times and no shared prefixes. Operators should measure $\mu(n)$ for the workload they serve, cap admitted sessions at the smallest population within a few percent of its peak, accepting that a burst then waits at the client, and re-measure when the workload, pool, engine or GPU changes.
