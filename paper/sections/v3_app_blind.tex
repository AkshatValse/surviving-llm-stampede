\section{The blind tests in detail}\label{app:blind}

This appendix gives the blind tests of \S\ref{sec:validation} and \S\ref{sec:control} in full, as scored: the frozen
predictions, the registered rules and their history, every outcome, and the frozen exploratory measures, hits and misses.
Table~\ref{tab:frozensum15} lists them with the other frozen predictions, and Table~\ref{tab:timeline-r15} gives their time
record. Section numbers in the text below refer to the main text.

\paragraph{Frozen predictions and scoring rules.} The predictions of Theorem~\ref{thm:trap} for runs that did not yet exist were frozen under tag
\texttt{icml-trap-pred} (commit \texttt{6b47a6c}, pushed at 00:24:47 CST on 2026-11-05 by GitHub's activity log), with scoring rules
S1--S5 declared beforehand: per-run composition within $\pm8$\% (pooled $\pm5$\%); pooled gap slope within $[0.6,1.4]\gamma$; completion
above $\lref$ in every trapped run at realized load $\ge1.1\lref$ (pooled within $\pm15$\% of $D$); pooled backlog slope within $\pm35$\%
of $\alpha$ or of the E2 fluid slope; and, per seed, the plug-in's side of the registered threshold and
$|$slope$-$plug-in$|\le0.01$\,/s. Theorem~1's application to a cell and load is refuted if the pooled S1 or S2 fails in a group of at
least two trapped runs (\fpath{notes/PREDICTIONS_icml_trap.md}). The tolerances were set after seeing the committed runs; the runs
they score did not exist at the push. S2 compares with $\gamma$, not with the completion-time slope $\gamma/z$; at 1.35~$\lref$ the
two differ by 1.3\%, well inside the tolerance.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \emph{TOOLSLOW-W} (pre-registered: tag \texttt{r15-reg}, commit \texttt{e05a262}, pushed at 03:18:36 CST, before the first job
was submitted at 03:21:38; designed after the 30\,s TOOLSLOW arm, whose slowdown ended at 2100\,s, trapped 1 of 3, and after
reading simulator outputs, so it is blind in outcome only): no burst; every tool call that starts in
$[1200,3000)$\,s takes 30\,s longer, at 1.35~$\lref$, seeds 0--2, paired with the committed NOBURST runs. The registered rule calls the
cell TRAPS if at least 2 of the 3 runs are stuck, NO-TRAP if at least 2 are good, and MIXED otherwise; its trapped runs, if any, are
scored by S1--S5. Outcome: TRAPS. All three runs were stuck (hit 0.000 over $[5700,8700)$\,s; backlog slopes 0.0192, 0.0184
and 0.0232\,/s against a threshold of 0.0125\,/s), while their paired NOBURST runs stayed good (hit 0.930--0.934); on the same
plans, the slowdown that ended at 2100\,s had trapped 1 of 3. All three runs are eligible for S1--S5, and the pooled scores pass:
composition $+1.9$\% (limit $\pm5$\%), gap slope $1.075\gamma$ (1.089 against the unregistered $\gamma/z$), completions $+1.9$\% of
$D$ with every run above $\lref$, and backlog slope $+29$\% of $\alpha$ (limit $\pm35$\%); Theorem~1's application to this cell is
not refuted. Per run, composition is within $\pm8$\% in all three (seed~1 at $+7.9$\%), and S5's plug-in lies on the measured side
of the threshold in 2 of 3 runs and within 0.01\,/s in 3 of 3. Every model we froze (the calibrated simulator in both modes and the
first-principles model) predicted this outcome, so the cell tests the trap-state law on a trap entered with no burst, not the models
against each other. Like TOOLSLOW's, its outcome shows that a temporary increase in tool latency, with no burst, can trigger the
trap; it does not separate the longer gap from the capacity loss and the larger population that come with it. Of the frozen
exploratory measures, the first 150\,s window below a follow-up hit of 0.5 fell at 1950, 1950 and 2100\,s, inside its predicted
band, and the sessions alive at 2100 and 3000\,s met their reference accuracy; the outstanding requests at 3300\,s missed it by 8 and
6 in seeds 0 and~2, and pred2's queue-free plan walk undercounted the sessions alive at 3000\,s by 7--18.
\item \emph{TOOLSLOW-D15} (pre-registered with TOOLSLOW-W under tag \texttt{r15-reg}; jobs 17256873--75 submitted at 03:57:14
CST; designed, like TOOLSLOW-W, after the 900\,s arm and after reading simulator outputs, so it is blind in outcome only):
no burst; every tool call that starts in
$[1200,2100)$\,s takes 15\,s longer, at 1.65~$\lref$, seeds 0--2, paired with the committed NOBURST runs, under the same three-way
rule; its stuck runs, if any, are scored by S1--S5. It was chosen so that our two frozen predictors disagree: the calibrated
simulator, run on the exact client plans, called seed~0 stuck and seeds 1 and~2 good (NO-TRAP) in both modes, while the
first-principles model's plan-averaged fluid verdict called every seed stuck (TRAPS); the model's per-seed layer made the
simulator's modal calls, but with $P(\text{NO-TRAP})=0.41$. Outcome: NO-TRAP, as the simulator said, seed by seed. Seed~0 was stuck
(hit 0.000; backlog slope 0.0258 against a threshold of 0.02325\,/s), and seeds 1 and~2 recovered to their controls' hit rates
(0.934 and 0.924, against 0.934 and 0.921). The fluid verdict was wrong, and the per-seed layer gave the outcome a low probability
(log score $-0.89$); over the six TOOLSLOW-W and TOOLSLOW-D15 runs the simulator was right in 6, the per-seed modal calls in 6
and the fluid verdict in 4. The stuck run passed S1--S4 (composition $+0.1$\%, gap slope $1.02\gamma$, completion $+10$\% of $D$,
backlog slope $-15$\% of $\alpha$); its S5 plug-in missed the 0.01\,/s tolerance by 0.0004\,/s, and with one eligible run the
refutation rule does not apply. Across the four blind runs trapped by a tool slowdown alone, S1--S4 pass in both cells, and S5 is close
in 3 of 4 and on the correct side in 3 of 4. Of the frozen exploratory measures, the sessions alive at 2100\,s were off by 4 in
seed~1 (reference accuracy 1), the outstanding requests at 3300\,s by 7 in seed~0 (reference 5), and seed~0's first 150\,s window
below a follow-up hit of 0.5 came one bin early; pred2's plan walk missed seed~0's sessions alive at 3000\,s by 51.
\item \emph{CUT} (a blind test of model~F's trajectory under an arrival cut, the setting of Corollary~\ref{cor:trapthr}): the
1.65~$\lref$ BURST protocol, then from 2400\,s arrivals cut to 1.15~$\lref$ (CUT-A, $\rho'=1.145$, inside $(\lmiss,D(\lambda))$) or
to 0.85~$\lref$ (CUT-B, $\rho'=0.846$), seeds 0--2. Model~F's predictions, driven by each seed's exact client plan, were frozen under
tag \texttt{icml-cut-pred} (commit \texttt{2f90267}, pushed at 02:25:43 CST on 2026-11-05; \fpath{notes/PREDICTIONS_icml_cut.md}).
Model~F predicts that all six runs stay stuck over $[5700,8700)$\,s; that CUT-A, although its rate is below the trapped throughput
of the same seed's uncut run, does not clear, its backlog dipping by less than 15\% on 300\,s bins and growing again in seeds 0 and 2
(predicted slopes $S$ of the backlog over $[3000,8700)$\,s of $+0.0039$ and $+0.0028$\,/s; seed~1's plan has few post-cut arrivals,
and its predicted slope is $-0.0026$\,/s); and that CUT-B's backlog shrinks slowly (predicted slopes $-0.0095$ to $-0.0131$\,/s) and would empty
only at about 27{,}100\,s, the busy period of Proposition~\ref{prop:classic}(a), far beyond the run.

The decision rule was registered after the predictions were pushed and before any CUT job (commit \texttt{11b0e4e}, tag
\texttt{r15-cut-reg}, pushed at 03:54:49 CST; jobs 17256867--72 submitted at 03:57). Per seed, the measured slope $S$ over
$[3000,8700)$\,s is compared with model~F's frozen slope and with two comparators: a naive one,
$\lambda'_{\mathrm{plan}}-D_{\mathrm{trap}}$, where $D_{\mathrm{trap}}$ is the trapped throughput of the same seed's uncut 1.65~$\lref$
control over $[3000,8700)$\,s, fixed beforehand at 0.0956, 0.1005 and 0.0946\,sessions/s (1.34, 1.41 and 1.32~$\lref$), the reading
in which a cut below the measured throughput drains the trap; and, for CUT-A, the quasi-static $\lambda'_{\mathrm{plan}}-\lref$, in
which the trap completes sessions at the all-miss capacity. From the frozen plans the naive comparator is about $-0.009$ to
$-0.020$\,/s for CUT-A and $-0.033$ to $-0.043$\,/s for CUT-B, and the quasi-static one $+0.009$ to $+0.015$\,/s for CUT-A, so model~F's
values lie well between them. A seed is theory-favoured if model~F's slope is strictly the closest to $S$, and a run that leaves the
trap counts against the theory. CUT-A passes if at least 2 of 3 seeds are theory-favoured and the slope over $[5700,8700)$\,s is
positive in at least 2 of 3 (model~F predicts that clause from seeds 0 and 2); CUT-B passes if at least 2 of 3 seeds are
theory-favoured and $S<0$ in at least 2 of 3. Either arm fails if a comparator is favoured, or the run leaves the trap, in at least
2 of 3 seeds; otherwise it is mixed. The
rule was fixed after the per-seed predictions had been read, and it differs from the draft rule simulated in the prediction note in
five ways: it adds the quasi-static comparator (stricter); where the note counted a run that leaves the trap as not
theory-favoured, the rule also counts it toward a fail (stricter); it takes $D_{\mathrm{trap}}$ from the frozen controls instead of
each run's own completion rate over $[1800,2400)$\,s (stricter for seeds 0 and~1, looser for seed~2); it adds $S<0$ in at least 2 of
3 to CUT-B (stricter); and it moves CUT-A's sign clause from $[3000,8700)$ to $[5700,8700)$\,s, as the note recommended (looser: in
the note's simulation this raised CUT-A's probability of passing from 0.82 to 0.99). The test has limits: the cut comes 1200\,s
after the burst, so it probes the young trap's transient and the start of its regrowth, not the asymptotic rate, and CUT-B cannot
show recovery within the run.

\emph{CUT-A outcome: pass.} With arrivals cut to a nominal 1.15~$\lref$ (realized 1.12--1.20~$\lref$ over $[3000,8700)$\,s; the
plans are thinned Poisson streams), below the trapped throughput of the same seeds' uncut runs (1.32--1.41~$\lref$), all three runs
stayed stuck to the end of the run, at zero hits over $[5700,8700)$\,s. Over $[3000,8700)$\,s the backlog's slope was $+0.0040$,
$-0.0022$ and $+0.0029$\,/s, against model~F's frozen $+0.0039$, $-0.0026$ and $+0.0028$, the naive comparator's $-0.0094$, $-0.0204$
and $-0.0099$ and the quasi-static one's $+0.0146$, $+0.0086$ and $+0.0132$, so all three seeds were theory-favoured; over
$[5700,8700)$\,s it was positive in seeds 0 and~2 and negative in seed~1, as model~F predicted for that plan. The draft rule gives
the same verdict. The secondary predictions frozen with model~F (tolerances declared in the prediction note, not part of the registered
rule) held as well: completions over $[5700,8700)$\,s within 0.001\,/s of model~F's per-seed values, the served prompt within 0.9\%, a
dip below the $[2100,2400)$\,s level of at most 11.0\% on 300\,s bins (frozen bound 15\%; on 10\,s bins, against the backlog at the
cut, up to 19.1\%), and more than 150 sessions present over the last 300\,s. Model~F's backlog levels ran 5--10 sessions high (186
against 176 over $[2100,2400)$\,s in seed~0).

The pass is narrower than Corollary~\ref{cor:trapthr}. Both comparators assume a constant throughput, so the registered rule
rejects only the two constant-throughput readings it was built against, in which the trap drains at the throughput it showed or completes sessions at
the all-miss capacity: over $[3000,8700)$\,s, completions equal to the nominal cut rate fit as well as model~F, and both match the
measured slopes to within the engine's run-to-run jitter (0.0001--0.0005\,/s, from the identical pre-cut parts of runs with the same
plan). One result singles out model~F: in the late window, scored post hoc against slopes frozen with the predictions, the
backlog's slope was $+0.0071$, $-0.0027$ and $+0.0131$\,/s against model~F's $+0.0069$, $-0.0036$ and $+0.0129$, while constant
completions at $D_{\mathrm{trap}}$, at $\lref$, at their midpoint or at the nominal cut rate each missed by about 0.004\,/s or more
(the smallest, 0.0040, at the nominal cut rate in seed~0). The completion rate there supports it, also post hoc: it varied with the
plan as model~F said (1.05, 1.18 and 1.10~$\lref$ against 1.04, 1.20 and 1.09), and no single constant rate comes within 0.004\,/s
of all three seeds, although the best one (0.080\,/s) stays within the frozen tolerance of 0.006\,/s. All three traps also
persisted to 8700\,s, which each constant reading above also predicts: the naive one still leaves about 50--130 sessions at the end,
far above the onset $n_b=32.7$. Persistence rules out only a drain at the completion rate seen just before the cut, 1.70, 1.65 and
1.21~$\lref$ over $[1800,2400)$\,s, which would have reached the onset near 6300\,s in seeds 0 and~1; that is the operator-facing
content of Corollary~\ref{cor:trapthr}, and on the engine it held within the run. CUT-A's three runs shared one node, and ``did not
clear'' means within 8700\,s.

\emph{CUT-B outcome: pass.} After the cut to 0.85~$\lref$ (realized 0.81--0.88~$\lref$ over $[3000,8700)$\,s) the backlog
fell, slowly, at $-0.0085$, $-0.0126$ and $-0.0124$\,/s over $[3000,8700)$\,s, against model~F's frozen $-0.0095$, $-0.0131$
and $-0.0123$ and the naive comparator's $-0.033$, $-0.043$ and $-0.034$, so all three seeds were theory-favoured and all three
slopes were negative. All three runs were still stuck at 8700\,s (hit 0.000 over $[5700,8700)$\,s; 140, 77 and 89 sessions present,
against model~F's 139, 79 and 94). Here persistence discriminates: from the backlog over $[3000,3300)$\,s, the naive reading would
have reached the hit-recovery onset $n_b=32.7$ at 5656--7245\,s, and a drain at the completion rate seen just before the cut (1.68,
1.65 and 1.23~$\lref$ over $[1800,2400)$\,s) at 4877--7598\,s; model~F's fluid empties only at about 27{,}100\,s, the busy period of
Proposition~\ref{prop:classic}(a). A reading in which the trap completes sessions at the all-miss capacity, not a registered comparator for
this arm, fits the slopes about as well: had it been one, seed~0 would have favoured it (error 0.00036 against model~F's
0.00099\,/s), and CUT-B would still have passed, 2 of 3. Model~F's distinctive prediction for CUT-B, completions below that capacity as the trapped
mix ages, held after the fact: 0.918, 0.932 and 0.974~$\lref$ over $[5700,8700)$\,s against model~F's 0.918, 0.955 and 0.979, with
served prompts of 10.9--11.5k tokens against 8.4--8.8k in the uncut runs, although at the frozen tolerance of 0.006\,/s the
all-miss capacity also lies (just) inside. The frozen secondary predictions held too: completions within 0.002\,/s, the served
prompt within 1\%, and a negative trend of the median gap in all three seeds. CUT-B's runs ran on two nodes, and none left the trap within 8700\,s.

Together, on the engine a cut below the trapped throughput but above the all-miss capacity left every run trapped, and a cut below
the all-miss capacity shrank the backlog only slowly, every run still trapped at 8700\,s: the pattern of Proposition~\ref{prop:classic}(a) and Corollary~\ref{cor:trapthr}. Each
arm's registered rule rejected the naive reading, and CUT-A's also the quasi-static one; what singles out model~F against
intermediate readings is post hoc: CUT-A's late-window slopes and CUT-B's completions below the all-miss capacity.
\end{itemize}
